Vezuna privacy policy

Updated November 1, 2026

Publisher and contact

Vezuna is published by Heitor Ribeiro. For privacy questions or support, contact heitor.ramon@gmail.com. This policy covers the Vezuna Android and iPhone apps and https://vezuna.app, including its documentation.

Your records

Vezuna stores household profiles, laboratory results, imported text, medications, schedules, dose events, health measurements, and local chat history on your device. You choose what to enter or import. Images and PDFs are processed on the device for text extraction. Check extracted values against the original document before confirming them.

Vezuna has no user account or publisher-operated health-record server. App code does not upload your health records, documents, prompts, or answers to the publisher. The publisher cannot remotely retrieve, restore, correct, or delete records in your local vault.

Health permissions

With your permission, Vezuna reads heart rate, steps, and weight from Health Connect on Android or HealthKit on iPhone. These measurements support your personal health record and are stored in the encrypted local vault. Access is read-only and imports happen when you request them. Vezuna does not write health data to these services, sell health data, or use it for advertising, marketing, or credit decisions.

You can deny or revoke health access in your device's Health Connect or Health settings. Manual records and exports remain available. Revoking permission stops future access but does not remove measurements already imported into Vezuna.

Other permissions

Camera and selected-image or document access let you import reports. Notification permission lets Vezuna show local medication reminders. Android receives reboot and time-change events to reschedule reminders. You can manage these permissions in device settings. Notifications may reveal medication information to anyone who can see your screen, depending on your notification settings.

Storage, retention, and deletion

The app encrypts its local vault. Android uses Android Keystore. iPhone uses CryptoKit and iOS file protection, with the encryption key stored as a protected file in the app's sandbox. Device security still matters. No independent security certification is claimed.

Records remain locally until you remove them with the available app controls or remove the app and its stored data through device settings. An imported record is a separate copy from the original document or health service. Removing Vezuna does not remove the original source, shared exports, or backups stored elsewhere.

Vezuna disables Android cloud backup and device transfer for app data. App-owned iPhone vault and model directories are marked as excluded from backup. In-app encrypted backups require the same device key to restore. They do not provide cross-device recovery. Uninstalling the app or losing the key can make a backup unusable.

Exports and sharing

You choose when to create or share PDF, CSV, and backup files through system controls. PDF and CSV files are not encrypted by Vezuna. Once shared, recipients and other apps control their copies. Review the destination before sharing. Vezuna cannot remove copies held by recipients or other services.

Local AI and store services

When a supported model is installed and activated, inference runs on the device. Generated answers can be wrong and are not medical advice. Model installation and AI activation are separate from access to your records and exports. Available model packs depend on the released version and device support.

Google Play and the App Store handle purchases, license verification, and any store-hosted asset downloads under their own terms. They may process store-account, transaction, device, and delivery information. Vezuna app code does not provide your health records to these services.

Third-party software

Android text recognition uses Google ML Kit with a bundled recognition model. Google's ML Kit disclosure describes diagnostic and usage information, including device and app information, installation identifiers, performance, API configuration, event types, and errors. The Android app removes its INTERNET permission, but platform and store services operate separately. See https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure and https://policies.google.com/privacy for Google's disclosures.

iPhone text recognition uses Apple's Vision framework. HealthKit, Health Connect, system file pickers, notification services, and sharing destinations have their own privacy controls and policies. See https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/ for Apple's privacy policy. Vezuna includes no app-owned advertising or analytics service and does not use your health data for tracking.

Website

The website provides marketing information and documentation. It has no health-record upload form, account system, newsletter, or app-owned advertising or analytics. Documentation search runs locally in your browser against a static index.

Vercel hosts the website and may process IP addresses, approximate location derived from IP, browser or system information, and request logs to deliver and protect the site. Its practices are described at https://vercel.com/legal/privacy-policy. External links take you to services with their own policies. Do not put medical information in website URLs or public GitHub issues.

Contact and requests

If you email the publisher, the email provider and publisher receive the address, message, and attachments you send for the purpose of responding. Do not send medical records, vaults, backups, passwords, or signing keys. Ask privacy questions or request access, correction, or deletion of your correspondence at heitor.ramon@gmail.com. Local app records must be managed on your device because the publisher has no copy of them.

Policy changes

Changes to this policy are published at https://vezuna.app/privacy with an updated date. Each app version includes an offline copy of its policy. Check the online policy for updates.
